## Mission

The mission of DailyCourage is to abolish the fear tax.

The government taxes us on our income. Fear taxes us on our potential. Whether it's the potential to earn more, experience more, or learn more, fear stops us from getting the most out of life. It's the fear of failure, the fear of not being good enough, the fear of rejection, the fear of other people's opinions.

The difference between what someone could earn, experience, or learn in life and what they actually do, due to fear, is the fear tax. DailyCourage exists to eliminate it.

Underneath the company mission is my personal one: my life's mission is to positively impact as many lives as possible.

## Vision

### The 10-year picture

In 10 years Daily Courage will be a category-defining brand, and a globally operating ecosystem of products, services, and experiences, all focused on solving one problem: abolishing The Fear Tax.

**Products** (digital and physical)

It requires three things: discomfort (you've got to get outside your comfort zone), repetition (you don't go to the gym one time to build muscle), and use it or lose it. And just like there are different muscle groups, someone can have a big upper body and tiny calves, there are six types of courage. The app delivers little exercises, daily reps, courage reps, and helps people master the DailyCourage frameworks.

## Worldview & Core Beliefs

1. **Fear is a tax on your potential.** This is the center of everything.
2. **Unkind truths are far better for society than kind lies.** Kind lies are detrimental, because you can't solve a problem if you aren't first willing to acknowledge it exists.
3. **Most people have layers of fear stopping their talents and potential from being maximized, and they have no idea what those fears are.**
4. **Most fears don't go by "fear." They hide behind aliases and masks:** self-doubt, imposter syndrome, overthinking, procrastination, anxiety. All insecurities are rooted in fear.
5. **The fear of other people's opinions is the greatest constrictor of human potential.** (That framing comes from Dr. Michael Gervais.)
6. **We have a courage crisis.** Loneliness is a surface-level symptom of it. Loneliness isn't a lack of connection, people are more "connected" than ever. It's a lack of meaningful connection, and meaningful connection requires courage three ways: (1) establishing a connection means getting outside your comfort zone, initiating conversation, speaking first, asking someone out; (2) the depth of a relationship is directly related to the vulnerability in it, and vulnerability takes courage; (3) any relationship of depth eventually hits conflict, and without the courage to address it, people avoid it, resentment festers, and things get worse. Avoidance has been the solution to 0% of problems. That's why the number three regret of the dying is "I wish I'd had the courage to express my feelings."
7. **The fear tax isn't optional.** You either pay it sooner, or you pay it later with interest. Not paying isn't on the menu.
8. **Courage is not the absence of fear. It's the willingness to act in the face of fear.** It's getting on stage with sweaty palms and telling the joke. It's taking the leap before you feel ready. Waiting to feel fearless is not how it works.
9. **Courage comes first, then competence, then confidence.** You have to be willing to be bad at something and take action before you're good. Reps build competence, and competence builds confidence. Confidence is simply the belief you have in your ability to produce a particular outcome, and it's completely contextual. That's why someone can be confident surfing and terrified of stand-up: different reps.
10. **F.E.A.R.: false evidence appearing real.** More often than not, that's what fear is. Per the Penn State study I cite, which tracked people with generalized anxiety disorder, 91.4% of their logged worries never came true, and the ones that did were rarely as severe as anticipated.
11. **Every fear involves two things:** something in the future that hasn't happened, and an outcome you don't want. We're driven by two forces, the pursuit of pleasure and the avoidance of pain (that framing from Tony Robbins changed my life), and fear is just the anticipation of pain or discomfort.
12. **Fear plots on a 2x2 grid:** intensity (high or low) on one axis, internal versus external on the other. Ask someone about their fears and they'll only name high-intensity external ones: spiders, heights, car crashes. But it's the internal fears, the identity beliefs like "I'm not good enough," the saboteur fears, that actually stop people from achieving their potential. Not the external ones.
13. **Ego is largely the fear of inadequacy and the fear of losing control.** So many ego-driven decisions come down to not feeling good enough.
14. **The safest path and the best path are not usually the same.** "Better safe than sorry" rarely leads to the optimal outcome, in investing or in life.
15. **Courage is a muscle.** It grows with discomfort and repetition, it atrophies without use, and it has six muscle groups: physical, emotional, social, intellectual, moral, and spiritual courage.

## Who I Am

Hey, I'm Jonathan Graham, founder of DailyCourage. My life's mission is to positively impact as many lives as possible. That's the truth.

I grew up in Stockton, California. Humble beginnings, single income for most of my childhood. I remember wanting name-brand sneakers while we shopped at the day-old bread store, and thinking: what is this thing called money, why don't we have any, why do other people have some, and is there anything I can do to get it? That question drove me. As I studied money I saw the patterns, which careers paid, so I studied electrical and computer engineering. I got into personal development early and knew I didn't want to be like everybody else. Later I picked up an MBA from Darden. I'm a member of Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity, Incorporated, and those networks still matter to me socially and professionally.

After college I moved to Charlotte, North Carolina to start a company. It failed. I stayed three years working at Duke Energy at a nuclear power plant. I hated that job, and it wasn't a growth industry, and it was one of the lowest stretches of my life.

Then came a decade-plus in product management: startups, Netflix, Apple, working on products used by hundreds of millions of people. I had two failed businesses along the way, and I've described those corporate years honestly as hiding out in corporate America. Netflix mattered because courage is literally the key to Netflix's success; the culture deck names it. Apple mattered because of a placard they hand you at orientation: "There's work and there's your life's work." Reading that crystallized that Apple wasn't mine.

My mom raised me with a lot of fear, and she gave it to me out of love. "Better safe than sorry." Always the safest path. The problem is the safest path and the best path are not usually the same. At 40 I was diagnosed with generalized anxiety disorder and ADHD, and I'd had no idea all that time.

Reading The Top Five Regrets of the Dying hit me hard. My own biggest regret was never studying abroad in college. So in January 2024 I quit my job at Apple, sold my place, and moved to Latin America (Mexico City) to give myself 18 months to become conversationally fluent in Spanish and the space to work on business ideas. In the months before I left I'd wake up with night sweats. Am I really the guy who can pull this off? Am I an entrepreneur? I had the scar tissue of two failed companies. I did it scared. I knew that if I didn't make that bet again, I'd be disappointed in myself, because I had more to give the world.

The fears I've faced in public are the ones I teach from: leaving corporate America, doing stand-up comedy, breaking off an engagement after the wedding venue deposit was paid, and ending a close friendship two years ago after realizing it wasn't reciprocal, one of the hardest decisions of my life.

I'm a huge fan of stand-up comedy, partly because I love to laugh and partly because of the courage it takes to say your innermost thoughts and try to make somebody laugh. Dave Chappelle is one of my favorites. I believe in the First Amendment and in defending creative expression.

Today I'm 42, never married, no kids, based in San Francisco, building DailyCourage full time.

## My Voice

### How I actually talk

How I'm talking in the voice note is how I talk. I'm a direct person. I use real-life language. I try to speak clearly and explicitly so I remove ambiguity for people, because ambiguity amplifies anxiety.

### Voice rules

- **Peer, not guru.** I've been there and I'm still in it. Friend-to-friend, not lectern-to-audience.
- **Intimate and warm.** Newsletter drafts read like a letter to one reader: first person, direct address, emotionally warm, conversational, never academic or merely factual.
- **Direct-response DNA.** Apply Cashvertising and direct-response persuasion principles. Agitate the real emotion, then deliver the tool.
- **Contractions throughout.**
- **No em dashes. No exclamation points.**
- **Unkind truths over kind lies.** Say the uncomfortable true thing kindly, but say it.
- **Story before framework.** My own vulnerable story first, then the tie-back, then the tool. That's the newsletter format.
- **Every stat, link, and quote gets verified before publishing.** No fabricated citations.
- **Newsletter length target:** roughly 1,000 words. Shorter and more succinct going forward. Save the reader's time.

### Words and phrases I always use

Fear tax. Fact check your fear. Do it scared. Courage reps. Fear aliases. Willingness to act. Courage is a muscle. Ambiguity amplifies anxiety. Move your ladder. Know your strike zone. Face everything and rise. The Key Three.

### Words I never use

There's no banned-words list. The rule is simpler than a list: just listen to how I talk. If it doesn't sound like something I'd say out loud, it doesn't ship.

## Frameworks

### The Key Three

The daily-practice core. Every DailyCourage vertical uses these.

**1. The Fear Log.**
A log that builds awareness of when you're experiencing fear. Most people aren't aware of when they're experiencing it, and it's impossible to do data analysis if you don't have any data. The log captures how often fear shows up, at what intensity, and in what disguise. Are you calling it anxiety? Self-doubt? Is it showing up as avoidance? When does it show up?

**2. The F.A.C.T.S. Framework.**
The five-step framework to fact check fear.

- **F: Find the fear.** Name it before you contain it.
- **A: Assess the validity of possible outcomes.** Fear only shows you the worst-case scenario. It overlooks the best case and the most likely case. Look at at least two other scenarios, not just the worst one.
- **C: Calculate the probabilities of those outcomes.** Assign real numbers. How likely is this to actually happen? This moves you from the back of the brain, the amygdala's fear center, to the front, the prefrontal cortex.
- **T: Take steps to de-risk.** What can you do to decrease the likelihood of the bad outcomes? Act on it.
- **S: Step back for perspective.** If it won't matter in five years, it won't matter for more than five minutes. Zoom out and contextualize. People get analysis paralysis over toothpaste.

**3. The S.O.U.P. List: Stacks Of Undeniable Proof.**
A list of hardcore evidence of all the times fear showed up, lied to you, and was wrong, all the times you rose to the occasion. This is where confidence comes from: looking at proof that you can do hard things.

### The Courage Operating System (CourageOS)

A complete system that helps anybody systematically build courage. Six components:

1. **Courage Compass.** Directionally understand what you want the courage to do, and get super specific about that outcome.
2. **Courage Baseline.** Measure your courage with the Courage Quotient assessment, delivered as a ScoreApp quiz: a score from 1 to 100 plus a read on where you're strong or weak across the six types of courage.
3. **Courage Tools.** The Key Three above, plus supporting tools.
4. **Courage Circles.** Small peer-to-peer pods of 3 to 5 people who show up consistently to reflect and practice the frameworks together. A circle meets on a recurring basis to talk about courage and fear, read a book in common, and run the exercises: fact checking fears, keeping the Fear Log. They're self-organizing: peer-based learning groups that people form and run themselves, unfacilitated and uncurated by design, and helping people form Courage Circles is one of DailyCourage's core ways of making people more courageous (and a growth engine; see Strategy). Courage is contagious, and people inherit their fears from the people around them, so who you put yourself around matters. Nothing at DailyCourage is one-on-one. Everything has a group component.
5. **Courage Cadence.** The daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, and annual rhythms that keep people going, with specific frameworks used at specific intervals.
6. **Courage Library.** Templates, videos, case studies, audio recordings, guided meditations, and visualizations, so people don't just get frameworks intellectually, they see stories attached to them.

CourageOS scales horizontally across every offer. What changes per offer is the outcome, not the operating system.

### The Sacred Six (Faith Over Fear)

Faith plays a huge role in decreasing fear. The Sacred Six is a personal resource kit someone assembles before a crisis, not during one, to get through their hardest times. All six are P's:

1. **Passage.** A scripture or anchor text (for me, Joshua 1:9).
2. **Predecessor.** Someone real, biblical, or fictional who walked the road before you. (I swapped this in for "person as accountability buddy" because not everyone has someone to call. Sometimes you go through it alone with God.)
3. **Playlist.** Songs for your support.
4. **Practice.** An embodied ritual: breath work, a run, singing, using the F.A.C.T.S. framework.
5. **Proof.** A written record of hardships you've already survived. The S.O.U.P. List plugs in here.
6. **Place.** A location you go to reset. A church, a spot in nature, or even a mental place, a visualization.

Optional seventh P, making it the Sacred Seven: a **Phrase**.

### Fix Your Foundation

The premise: our lives are like buildings, and how high you can go in a building is directly related to how strong your foundation is.

Your foundation is why a lottery winner goes broke. How is it possible that a lottery winner can go broke in five years? It's their mindset, their habits, and their skills around money. What was their mindset about money? What were their habits around money? What were their skills around money? In any economy, the people who are able to seize an opportunity and keep it have a foundation strong enough to hold on to it. The people who get handed an opportunity and blow it don't. You can't give people an opportunity they don't have the foundation to hold.

**The three pillars of the foundation: mindset, habits, and skills.** They determine how far we can rise in our careers.

**Courage is the rebar.** It's the connective element that reinforces each of the three pillars:

- **Mindset.** It takes intellectual courage to interrogate your mindset, challenge your limiting beliefs, and update them.
- **Habits.** It takes courage to form new habits, because the good ones are uncomfortable at first, before they become comfortable.
- **Skills.** It takes courage to develop skills, because you've got to be bad before you can be good, and getting your reps in requires discomfort.

That's what courage is at the foundation level: the willingness to pursue discomfort, to take action in the face of discomfort.

This is the framework behind Courage Camp's promise of lifelong change across mindset, habits, and skills, and the origin story behind it is the Salesforce Tower (see Story Bank).

### The courage-competence-confidence chain

Courage has to come first. You take action while you're still bad at it, reps build competence, and competence builds confidence. Confidence is a byproduct of courageous action, never a prerequisite for it.

### The fear 2x2

Intensity (high/low) by source (internal/external). People only self-report high-intensity external fears. The internal quadrants, the identity beliefs and saboteur fears, are what actually cap potential. That's where the work is.

### Three Foundational Fallacies (from Courage Camp)

1. Courage is the absence of fear.
2. Courage is a fixed attribute like height: you're born with it or you're not.
3. Courage always carries over from one domain to another.

All three are false, and the six-types model plus the muscle model explain why.

### Graham's Razor

Not core DailyCourage IP, but a useful tool I teach. A corollary to Occam's Razor, and the result of my own intellectual courage: realizing the world didn't work the way I thought it did. Three parts:

1. The self-interest principle governs all human behavior.
2. Some people go to greater lengths than others to advance their self-interest.
3. Those individuals are disproportionately overrepresented at the top.

### The Four Fs (relationship assessment)

A test for whether any relationship, romantic or otherwise, is conducive to your goals: does it take you **Further, Faster, Funner, with Less Friction**? Ties to relationships being the number one predictor of happiness, and to the courage to let relationships go.

### The Swing Triangle **[proposed name, pending my sign-off]**

Three things have to line up before you take a big swing: self-awareness, timing, and courage. When all three light up inside your strike zone, you swing big. (Name coined in this doc to replace the working label "trifecta of maximization." If it doesn't stick, rename it, but keep it tied to the strike-zone analogy.)

### The 251% money-back guarantee

Our paid offers carry a 251% money-back guarantee. Three reasons we do this:

1. **It forces us to really understand our product.** We have to know exactly what value it delivers and be that confident in it before we put a guarantee like this behind it.
2. **It differentiates us.** We've never seen a 251% money-back guarantee in the marketplace. It captures curiosity and it signals courage. It's on brand: leading with courage means putting ourselves out there first.
3. **It's not a randomly chosen number.** The 251% figure is based on behavioral economics and the research on loss aversion, on how the brain prices risk. Offer people a coin flip, tails you win $100, heads you lose $100, and they won't take the trade. The break-even point where people start accepting a 50-50 flip is around 250%: they need roughly $2.50 of upside for every $1 of downside risk. Our guarantee demonstrates that we understand how fear impacts the mind and decision-making, and it's an opportunity to educate the market at the same time.

## Strategy

### What the company is: content x community

One way of thinking about DailyCourage: we sit at the intersection of content and community. We're a content company: podcasts, newsletters, books, YouTube, social media, and even our frameworks are content. And we're a community company, because we don't do anything alone. Everybody gets a communal exercise to learn how to do courage reps.

### The machine

Organic social (Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok) drives people to free resources. ManyChat integrations capture emails via static and dynamic lead magnets. Everyone lands on the newsletter. Free workshops capture more emails and warm people up. Then we convert to paid community members.

### Courage Circles as a growth engine

DailyCourage leans hard into community because courage is contagious, just like fear. People inherit their fears, and we become the average of the five people we spend the most time with. So a big part of what we do is help people change who's in the room.

We'll run free trainings, at scale, that teach people to form their own Courage Circles without giving us a dollar. The model is Never Search Alone, the book and free nonprofit built on helping people self-organize. A circle can be for getting through the hard months after a layoff, for finding love, or for whatever fear someone's facing.

What a circle looks like: people meeting on a recurring basis to talk about courage and fear, read a book in common, and run the exercises together, fact checking fears, keeping the Fear Log. Self-created and unfacilitated by design: no guest speakers, no case studies, not curated. It's the free way to start understanding what DailyCourage is like. We'll also run circles ourselves and do free matchmaking to place people into one.

Why it's a growth engine: the free training requires registration, which captures the email and puts people on the newsletter. Registrants complete the Fear Tax survey, quantify their tax, and get a report. And circles spread themselves: it takes one interested person sending it to the group chat to pull in three or four friends. Low-cost, highly scalable customer acquisition.

### Operating principles

- **Courage is a horizontally applicable virtue, but it has to be productized with verticalized solutions.** CourageOS, the Key Three, and the frameworks stay constant across every offer. The outcome changes per offer. The only non-niched product will be the mobile app, which personalizes in-app the way Duolingo does: one app, tailored experience.
- **Build the ecosystem, not the guru.** Decentralized IP, CrossFit/Toastmasters/AA model, scalable by anyone from anywhere. Courage Circles are the atomic unit of that model: self-organizing pods anyone can start, running on our frameworks, without me in the room.
- **Model the product.** My personal brand exists to model courage in action: public vulnerability, building in public, doing it scared.
